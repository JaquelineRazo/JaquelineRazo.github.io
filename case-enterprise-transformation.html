Enterprise Transformation & Investment Strategy

From scattered initiatives to a structured transformation agenda.

Enterprise Transformation
Role Transformation & Business Case Lead
Context Global consumer organization
Focus Business Cases · Investment Prioritization · Measurement

The Context

Organizations often invest in innovation across multiple capabilities without a consistent way to determine what should be prioritized, how success should be measured, or how each initiative contributes to business value.

I worked across a portfolio of transformation initiatives spanning customer data, digital commerce, measurement, search and brand-building — each pitched, funded and judged on its own terms.

The Challenge

Scattered initiatives

Innovation investments spanned multiple capabilities with no shared framework for evaluating them against each other.

No consistent value language

Teams pitched projects in different terms, making it hard to compare a data platform against a brand campaign.

Unclear ownership

Cross-functional initiatives often lacked a single accountable owner once funded.

Measurement came late

Success metrics were frequently defined after a project launched, not before it was approved.

My Role

My Role Discover → Structure → Design → Build → Implement

Discover — mapped the existing initiative landscape across customer data, digital commerce, measurement, search and brand-building.

Structure — built a common framework for describing expected value, effort and risk across dissimilar initiative types.

Design — designed a prioritization model and a lightweight business-case template teams could use consistently.

Build — stood up the scoring and prioritization process as a repeatable operating rhythm, not a one-time exercise.

Implement — rolled the framework out across the initiative portfolio and supported stakeholders through their first cycle using it.

The Approach

The Approach Business Goals → Opportunity Areas → Prioritization → Execution → Measurement → Value

What I Built / Changed

A common business-case template

Every initiative, regardless of type, gets evaluated on the same expected-value, effort and risk terms.

A prioritization model

Turns “which of these projects should we fund” from a political conversation into a structured one.

Measurement defined upfront

Success metrics get attached before an initiative is approved, not after.

Outcome

Comparable initiatives

Projects across different capabilities became comparable for the first time, instead of being judged in isolation.

Structured prioritization

Funding decisions became repeatable rather than ad hoc, case-by-case negotiations.

Earlier measurement

Fewer initiatives launched without a defined way to judge success.

What This Project Taught Me

The hardest part of enterprise transformation usually isn't picking the right initiative — it's building a shared language sturdy enough that a data platform and a brand campaign can be judged by the same standard. Once that language exists, prioritization stops being a negotiation and starts being a calculation.
