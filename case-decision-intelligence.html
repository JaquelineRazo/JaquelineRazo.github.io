Decision Intelligence
Role Analytics & Measurement Strategy
Context Large U.S. consumer business
Focus Measurement · Forecasting · Executive Reporting

The Context

Data only becomes useful when it changes a decision.

Across advanced analytics and measurement initiatives, I have worked on the systems that connect business questions with data, models, learning agendas and executive decision-making.

The Challenge

Reporting without a question

Dashboards existed, but weren't always built to answer a specific business decision.

Fragmented measurement

Different teams measured performance differently, making cross-comparison difficult.

Slow feedback loops

Insight often arrived after the decision it should have informed.

No shared learning agenda

Testing and experimentation weren't coordinated toward a common set of open questions.

My Role

My Role Discover → Structure → Design → Build → Implement

Discover — identified the business questions executives actually needed answered, not just the metrics already being reported.

Structure — designed a measurement strategy and KPI architecture connecting those questions to data and models.

Design — built forecasting and reporting approaches suited to how decisions were actually made.

Build — implemented the measurement infrastructure and executive-reporting workflow.

Implement — established a learning agenda so testing and analysis compounded over time instead of resetting each quarter.

The Approach

The Approach Business Question → Data → Measurement / Model → Learning → Decision → Next Test

What I Built / Changed

A KPI architecture

Tied to specific business questions, not just whatever data happened to be available.

Forward-looking measurement

Forecasting approaches that inform decisions before they're made, not just after.

An executive reporting rhythm

Designed around what leaders needed to decide, not everything that could be reported.

Outcome

Consistent methodology

Investment-effectiveness questions answered with a repeatable approach instead of ad hoc analysis each time.

Reporting used to decide

Reporting became something used to decide, not just something reviewed.

Compounding learning

A repeatable learning agenda meant each test built on the last, rather than starting over.

What This Project Taught Me

Measurement work is often treated as a reporting exercise. The more valuable version of it is a learning system — one that tells you not just what happened, but what to test next. That reframe changes what gets built, and who ends up using it.
